**The Conflict:**
1.  **GPLv2 (The Linux Kernel):** "If you modify this code and distribute it, you must share your changes."
    * *The Loophole:* **TiVo**. TiVo used the Linux kernel (GPLv2). They shared their source code changes. If you compiled your own version of Linux and tried to run it on the TiVo, the hardware refused to boot.
    * *The Result:* You had the code, but you couldn't run it. You had "Software Freedom" but not "Hardware Freedom." This is **Tivoization**.

2.  **GPLv3 (The RMS Response):** Stallman wrote GPLv3 to close this loophole. It says: "If you use this code, you cannot prevent the user from running their own modified versions on your hardware." You must give them the "installation keys."

3.  **The Schism:**
    * **Stallman/FSF:** Adopted GPLv3. (GCC, Emacs, etc.)
    * **Linus Torvalds:** **REJECTED GPLv3.** He kept Linux on GPLv2 "forever."
    * **Why?** Linus argued that his job is to ensure the *software* remains free. He believes he has no right to dictate how a hardware manufacturer builds their device. If Sony wants to build a PlayStation that runs Linux but locks it down to prevent piracy, Linus is okay with that, *as long as Sony gives back the kernel improvements*.

**The Consequence:**
Because Linux stayed on GPLv2, it conquered the world. It is in your Android phone, your Tesla, your TV, and the Supercomputers. If Linux had moved to GPLv3, companies like Google, Samsung, and NVIDIA might have abandoned it for a BSD-licensed kernel (like FreeBSD) to protect their hardware locks.
Linus traded "User Sovereignty over Hardware" for "Global Ubiquity of the Kernel."
