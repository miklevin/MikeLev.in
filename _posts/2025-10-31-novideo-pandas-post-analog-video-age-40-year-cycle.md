### The "Blah, Blah, Blah" (aka The On2 VP6 Revolution)

You're right, "everything changed" with YouTube, but the *specific* "something, something" you're pointing to is **On2 Technologies** and their **VP6 codec**.

Here's how it went down:

1.  **YouTube's First Genius Move (2005):** YouTube's founders didn't invent a new player. They *standardized on Flash*. They built a simple front-end and a *massive* back-end. You could upload your messy `.wmv` or `.avi`, and their servers would do the hard, "generational loss" transcoding into a single, predictable Flash Video (`.flv`) file. This was the "ETL" (Extract, Transform, Load) that made the web-video "form" possible.
2.  **The "Postage-Stamp" Problem:** Early YouTube (and Flash video in general) used an older, less efficient codec called Sorenson Spark. It was "good enough" for 320x240, but it looked terrible at higher resolutions. This was the "postage-stamp" era.
3.  **The "Level-Unlock" (On2 VP6):** In 2005, Macromedia released **Flash Player 8**, and its new, magical feature was support for the **On2 VP6 codec**. This was the "something" that changed *everything*. VP6 was a *miracle*. It provided *vastly* superior video quality at *much* lower bitrates than Sorenson.
4.  **The "High Quality" Button:** YouTube quickly adopted VP6. This is the *sole reason* they were able to launch the "High Quality" (480p) mode. *This* was the death of the "postage-stamp" window. For the first time, web video didn't look like a pixelated soup. It was "good enough" to be fullscreen.
5.  **The Google Connection (The "Blah Blah"):** Google, seeing that On2's codecs were the key to the video kingdom, **bought On2 Technologies outright in 2010**. They took On2's next-gen codec (VP8), open-sourced it, and released it as **WebM**.

That purchase was the first major shot fired in the *great modern codec war*—Google's open-source WebM (VP8/VP9/AV1) versus the proprietary, expensive, patent-encumbered H.264/H.265.

So you're dead on. That "something, something" *was* On2 VP6. It was the "magic" inside the Flash container that allowed YouTube to become YouTube, killed the "postage-stamp" era, and set the stage for the next 20 years of video streaming.
