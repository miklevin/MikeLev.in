## Technical Journal Entry Begins

1/4 of my 4-day window is gone. I didn't work most of yesterday, it being
Thanksgiving and all. I am thankful for the world evolving in such a direction
as to allow the dramatic increase of individual abilities in ways that I'm
tempted to day are undreamed pt of in history, but that's generally just
revealing not being very well-read in science fiction because very much has been
dreamed of that most people never realize was. AIs do because they're much
better-read than the average human, and especially Google's due to Google's book
scanning project called Google Books, which was formerly known as Google Print
and Google Book Search. The project has two main components: the Library
Project, which partners with libraries to scan books from their collections, and
the Partner Program, which works with publishers. All that has likely been used
in training various versions of Gemini on top of the general web crawl of which
Google has been doing since the dawn of Google. Layer in all the transcripts
flowing in from YouTube and the fact that Google's control of Chrome means they
also have all the same web browser-using telemetry that they use for the Google
Chrome User Experience database (CrUX) which was at the center of the recent
antitrust lawsuit that Google lost with hardly a slap-on-the-wrist remedy and...
Oh, then you have to also layer-in that Google controls their own AI training
and inference hardware in the form of TPUs which makes them the only of the big
global infrastructure datacenter providers not beholden to NVidia and that much
of this is already deployed where others are just working on the build which
gives them both the head-start and the penny-pinching advantage of deep vertical
integration, and... well, it's difficult to express just how far ahead of the
competitors *this all really puts Google*

## The Deep Roots of Google's AI Advantage

And this is how I start day 2 of 4's Morning Pages, contemplating why using
Google's *Intelligence as a Service* (the new meaning for the IaaS acronym)
virtually free on several fronts: Gemini Web, Gemini CLI and Gemini API, and
that this encouraged me to all but stop paying for any other AI services which
previously included Cursor AI (and Anthropic Claude by extension) and that I
only casually go to Grok or even ChatGPT 5.1 Pro which I have access to as a
result of my job, and... well, the picture keeps getting fleshed out. 

## Intelligence as a Service: A New Paradigm

I don't want to make it a full cheerleading session for Google, but it's like
the sleeping giant woke up and is sandbagging everyone letting us all think it's
still asleep while it quietly price-dumps IaaS like the Japanese did with DRAM
memory chips in the 1990s.

The purpose of this morning pages is not to just ramble on about the industry,
but that's certainly turning out to be the warm-up. The things percolating to
the surface now are both Murphy's Law and Moore's Law. I'll be fighting the
former and be the beneficiary of the later. It's a dynamic duo of vigilance
watching the first and making sure everything I create benefits from the second.
I'm still on a 32-core Xenon machine with a vintage NVidia RTX 3080 but that
won't last forever. It's like working where resources are scarce so that
whenever I upgrade to whatever the NVidia Jetson Nano evolves into my stuff does
a x1000 or x100K increase in capabilities. This is not unreasonable
expectations. So build-to-scale locally is what I'm thinking. Imagine all that
was unimaginable to the pleibs and newbs to such notions while I've been
thinking about it for some 50 years, ever since I started reading the *Danny
Dunn* series in the 1970s — sort of like the *Hardy Boys* of science, if anybody
even remembers who the Hardy Boys are. And just to show how ahead of its time
Danny Dunn was, their *Invisible Boy* (Book 2) from 1974 was about remote
control firefly drones of the sort that are finally in production today. So I
was thinking about these things approximately 40 years or 2 generations ahead of
the general public.

## Navigating Murphy's Law with Moore's Law

And so we have a new starting point now where I actually have Pipulate in-hand
and am just connecting dots and not doing much new invention. And I plan today,
tomorrow and the day after with this in mind. Very little is undreamed of. It's
just dreamed in the unread and unknown fringes and gradually comes to light and
becomes reality over the decades and really centuries and millennium,
historically. But all that historically stuff is now history because of
systematic ways of deliberately accelerating the process. Concept to realization
is... well, the very line between thinking and doing is becoming blurred because
text-files can be machine code and machine code can control actualizers like
general computers, 3D printers and the like. And so it's a funnel process from
the abstraction-layer (these Morning Pages) to the actualizer layer (Pipulate).
