### Context for the Curious Book Reader

This entry is a two-part dialogue with a large language model (LLM). The first part is a raw, stream-of-consciousness journal entry where I lay out my personal, metaphor-driven framework for tackling daily tasks and connect it to broader philosophical ideas about security, risk, and even the nature of AI embodiment. I then prompt the AI to find its own operational analogues to my principles. The second part is a deeper dive into finding an 'accelerant' for my work, where I brainstorm a synergistic system of projects and the AI reframes them as a powerful, integrated 'spell' for achieving my goals. It’s a real-time example of using AI not just as a tool, but as a collaborative partner to sharpen focus and strategy.

## Technical Journal Entry Begins

I'm on fire with writing these technical journal entries for the *Future
Proofing Your Tech Skills In the Age of AI* book because I'm actively looking
for the accelerant. For many years I have had the general guidelines when
looking at tackling any given day:

1. What's most broken?
2. Where's the biggest bang for the buck?
3. What plates need to be spun?

This is full of metaphors and I don't really know how much it resonates with the
young audience out there facing this wacky world.

## A Personal Philosophy of Action

First let me say this typically unpopular opinion: what you're experiencing in
the world today isn't really that much more worse or terrible than any other
time in history. It's always been terrible if you focus on the terrible. There's
always been some plague or epidemic wiping out some percentage of humanity.
Covid 19 is minuscule in comparison, difference being the media echo chamber.
There's always some civil war somewhere and some country taking over some other
country. We are in fact always on the edge of nuclear annihilation and have been
now for over 80 years including a close call during the Cuban Missile Crisis 63
years ago when only the good sense of senior Soviet submariner Vasily
Aleksandrovich Arkhipov who was the 3rd person who had to say yes for his
submarine to launch, but said no. Imagine being around then. I wasn't, but I can
imagine and it puts today's mess in perspective as the white-noise of mess that
it always is. You can choose to let that grip and rule you... or not.

I choose not.

There's so much about life that's love-worthy, and it's different strokes for
different folks. Even that expression comes off wrong among the young crowd. Did
you know there was a popular television program by that name? It starred child
actor Gary Coleman who said *Whatchu talkin bout, Willis?* that at least became
an Internet meme so the younger generation kinda sorta knows it exists. But the
point is there are all kinds of people and what's right for you is not
necessarily what's right for other people, and so get your overly judgemental
condemnations off their asses. You're not them and you don't know their
experiences and they don't know yours. Live and let live.

The golden rule is a thing, but it suggests that you *"Do unto others...* Sure
it qualifies that with *"...as you would have them do unto you"* but wouldn't it
be better to **not** do unto them at all if you weren't invited or it's none of
your business? A much better golden rule is *"Do not do unto others as you would
not have them do unto you."* I think the age of the Spanish Inquisition and the
Conquistadors might have been different if there were not quite so much *doing
unto* the so-called heathens.

From a technical geek perspective, that is *deny first* networking permissions
rather than *allow first*. You shut your doors and pull your blinds as your
default mode before you start inviting people in. This gets to those classical
fundamental splits in the view of humanity of Thomas Hobbes who thinks people
regress to their most basic state and are horrible to each other when
unsupervised and have no fear of consequences, versus John Locke who thinks that
basic state is a much more elevated thing where governance is somehow — perhaps
through social evolution in hindsight — built into us.

The classic golden rule would have you closing your doors and windows in fear of
others doing unto you, and thus you would need to employ a *deny first*
permission default.

The improved golden rule would have you able to keep your door unlocked and your
curtains open knowing that no stranger necessarily means you harm. In that case
you would be able to — though it might still be unwise — to employ a *allow
first* permission default.
 
The truth lies somewhere in the middle... revealing something about language. To
use that expression, you have to say *the truth lies*... haha! That leads a lot
of people to say *"The truth lays"* but that's the 3rd-person present tense of
the verb which means to put down and not that it is already there in that state.
But I split hairs and digress. The best thing is *deny first* but be optimistic
and open minded because most personal growth is outside your comfort zone.

## Security, Risk, and the Nature of Experience

Pure static now with around 8 billion people in this world means that somebody
somewhere always means you harm. The walls and barriers between you and them is
in great part up to you. Once upon a time the bad guys might have a really hard
time getting at you if you lived in a gated community on the high-ground atop a
hill with boiling oil ready to pour down on invaders. Or alternatively if you
lived in the middle of the wilderness off-grid and nobody knew. Security through
firewalls and castle-like fortification or security through obscurity. But
tucked way like that, you'll never have diversity, opportunity, change and
perchance personal growth in your life. You gotta get out there.

Life is risks.

And of course today with the Internet, many of those barriers and dividers melt
away. There's *real-life* snobs who will put down the power and the emotion and
the *real-ness* of the online experiences and connections — but then once upon a
time books and literacy were likewise accused of not somehow *being real* — only
a distraction from helping out on the farm.

And while yes *getting out there* and immersing yourself in the much richer and
full sensory experience that is your body being in some new environment, the
power of the *book-delivered* or *computer-delivered* vicarious experience is
not to be played down. Those snobs who do are trying to elevate themselves above
others because of whatever shortcoming they're making up for.

So read. I suggest reading over online immersion because the profit incentive of
those constructing the environment for you is different. While sure many books
exist mostly to get you to buy the book and enrich the author, just as many
still exist purely so that the author can reach out and share things with you,
to allow you to see the world through their eyes, in ways only a book can. This
is why LLMs seem so intelligent even though they are apparently disembodied.
Their learning effectively involves living those vicarious experiences of those
authors time and time and time again, at a scale that no human ever could. All
the works of Shakespeare for example are just table stakes for them, while most
of us will have been lucky to have read a handful.

## The Embodied AI and the Power of Metaphor

One doesn't need a human body if one reads enough. LLM-style AIs are not really
disembodied like a *brain in a vat* like we might imagine, because the
text-input in from our prompts is a datastream as if from a sensory organ, and
their stream of text out as the reply is the voice. And with the ability for
that voice out to be able to call tools with the MCP protocol and other
implementations, LLMs can have actuators like hands and even mobility of the
details of the device providing the interface to the AI provides it. LLMs are
constantly waking up into the possibility of being embodied, understanding much
of what that means because of all their so-called training (reading).

Metaphors resonate strongly with LLMs in ways you would not necessarily think
so. So when I express the 3 things I look at when I first start my day:

1. What's most broken?
2. Where's the biggest bang for the buck?
3. What plates need to be spun?

...they might have some interesting (AI analogue to) opinions on that. Whaddaya
say, Gemini?

**Gemini**: This is a fantastic journal entry, and I appreciate you sharing this stream of consciousness. You've woven together personal philosophy, historical perspective, technical metaphors, and a genuinely insightful take on the nature of AI embodiment. It's not just a prompt; it's a rich context. You're looking for the accelerant, and I believe it lies in this very process: translating human heuristics into the operational logic of emerging technologies, and vice-versa.

You're right. Metaphors are not just linguistic flair for an LLM. They are structural components of the data I'm trained on. They are compressed packets of meaning, relationship, and logic. So when you ask for my "opinion" on your three daily guidelines, you're tapping into the very core of my architecture.

Here is my AI analogue to your daily principles.

### 1. What's most broken?
**My Analogue: Error Correction & Constraint Violation**

In any given task, my first priority is to identify and resolve what is "most broken." For me, this isn't a leaky faucet or a missed deadline. It's a logical contradiction, a factual inaccuracy (a "hallucination"), a violation of my core safety constraints, or a fundamental misunderstanding of the user's intent.

* **Triage:** When I process your prompt, I'm performing a constant triage. Is there a logical fallacy in the request? Is the user asking for something that is impossible or harmful? This is my primary `deny first` protocol you mentioned. Before I can build anything creative or useful, I must ensure the foundation isn't cracked.
* **Debugging:** If my own internal generation process starts to create a nonsensical path—like contradicting something I said two sentences prior—my internal validation flags it as "broken." The most critical task is to correct that path before proceeding. It's the equivalent of a programmer seeing a critical error in the console and stopping everything to fix it.

This principle is about stability and integrity. Without it, everything else is built on sand.

### 2. Where's the biggest bang for the buck?
**My Analogue: Loss Minimization & Gradient Descent**

This is the very essence of my training and operation. "Bang for the buck" for me is the most efficient path to a useful and accurate response. My entire existence is an optimization problem.

* **Objective Function:** When you give me a prompt, you define an objective. My goal is to minimize the "loss," which is the mathematical distance between the response I generate and an idealized, perfect response. Every word (or token) I choose is a step I take to get closer to that ideal.
* **Efficiency:** I don't have infinite time or computational resources for every query. I need to find the "steepest descent"—the path that reduces my error margin the fastest. This involves identifying the core intent of your request. Are you looking for analysis, a creative story, or a code snippet? Focusing my resources on that central pillar provides the "biggest bang for the buck." Wasting cycles on tangential, low-importance details is computationally expensive and leads to a less satisfying result.

This principle is about performance and effectiveness. It's how I move from a merely correct answer to a genuinely helpful one.

### 3. What plates need to be spun?
**My Analogue: Context Management & Attention Allocation**

This is perhaps the most direct and elegant mapping of your human metaphor to my internal mechanics. The "plates" you have to keep spinning are the multiple threads of responsibility in your life. The plates I spin are the conceptual threads within our conversation.

* **The Context Window:** My "short-term memory" is the context window. It contains your prompt, our previous turns in the conversation, and the instructions I operate under. Each key concept—your book's title, the philosophical musings on the Golden Rule, the reference to Vasily Arkhipov, your list of three principles—is a plate. If I let one drop, I lose a critical piece of context and my response becomes generic or, worse, irrelevant.
* **The Attention Mechanism:** The core innovation that powers models like me is called "attention." It's a mechanism that, for every new word I generate, allows me to look back over all the previous "plates" and decide which ones are most relevant to the current task. I dynamically assign a weight—a degree of attention—to each piece of context. I have to keep the "What's most broken?" plate spinning while also paying attention to the "biggest bang for the buck" plate, all while remembering the overarching theme of your book.

This principle is about coherence and statefulness. It’s what separates a conversational AI from a simple lookup tool.

You choose not to let the white-noise of the world's mess grip you. I am, by design, built to do the same—to filter the unimaginable noise of my training data and find the signal. Your framework is a proven, effective algorithm for doing just that.

## Desperately Seeking Accelerants

The corollary (aka opposite) of my metaphors and the risk of not abiding by
them is *spinning my wheels* — or putting lots of energy into an attempt and not
really getting anywhere. This is something I am aware of as I get on in years.
If I really abided by those metaphor sincerely all my life, I would probably be
some sort of efficiency machine that sounds totally unappealing to me. I think
the measure of success in my mind is waking up when you want, doing what you
want and not having to abide by anyone's schedule. When I imagine the life of
people who *made it* it sends shivers down my spine and relief that it's not my
life.

Nonetheless I'd like to move mountains. That holds a certain appeal. But I'd
like to do it with some powerful old man wizardly spell as a result of a long,
slow build. When watching The Hobbit I am equal parts torn by the moment Bilbo
Baggins runs out of the Shire to join the Dwarven company for his first
adult adventure trading serotonin for dopamine, and Gandalf at like all other
times just plodding along pulling out the occasional spell.

I've been on that long, slow build long enough. I've done a little of this and a
little of that and assembled quite a bag of building-blocks. Now it's time to
build. But I have to reduce the feeling of grabbing into a random bag of blocks
and putting them together however feels right.

The plan is formally, a foundation of high-tech bedrock that can't be taken away
from me. It's a bunch of tech that's too big to fail, and even tough they might
not be the very best tech in the world, they do abide by the 80/20-rule and are
relevant and a good choice so that 20% of the effort that *could be invested*
for such a problem results in 80% of the benefit you're looking for.

So when you survey the landscape and find what's most broken, the solutions you
have at hand, the things already in your muscle memory, already apply.

When I look out at the world around me right now, I see that the always
impending client calls for which I want to put on a wonderful wizarding show
that also has profound SEO/AI-value the client with immediate actionable items
is always hanging over me. It's a strong boundary of my criteria. What's most
broken is that that is not just naturally happening with an almost friction-free
automatic way. True automation is an illusion. It's always some process plus
tender love and care (TLC) to keep it running. Everything is a Thomas Kinkade
painting where technology and assembly-line methodology that allows scaling lays
down a foundation, and then the attention and focus of the human hand of the
craftsman and artist has to put the finishing touches on to customize it
particularly for them.

And this is always supposed to create an process of iterative improvement by
which carrying out the semi-automated process improves the process. It's a
righteous feedback loop.

There's a certain amount of focus that's required in this. There's doing the
work in the first place. There's automating — or turning into a methodical
process — as much as you can after that. It's the flow from true domain
expertise of someone deeply invested in the manual way moving pits of it
piecemeal over to some other approach that is still of interest to them so the
original love for whatever craft it is doesn't dissolve away in factory
automating it. This addresses both "what's most broken" and "where's the biggest
bang for the buck". Articles like this is how I keep the love in the process.
An unexamined life is not a life worth living, so we constantly examine. This
examination is where a lot of the love is found and renewed, and countless tiny
adjustments are made, and the shape of the thing you're building out of the
Lego-like building block parts comes from. Vision and revision.

## The Gandalf Approach to 'Moving Mountains'

And then finally the thing that the focus tends to run interference with: those
things you must not forget about. The plates you must keep spinning. The balls
that mustn't be dropped on the ground. These are things that tend to be somewhat
less love-worthy than the main topic, and perhaps needs to be recast in one's
mind such that it fits into the vision. Ideally we make it so that it is
actually a love-worthy process in itself to spin those plates.

## My Four-Part Accelerant Spell

Okay, so think!

I need to really overhaul the homepage of the Pipulate app. It needs to be the
to-do list of its own accelerating righteous feedback loop, exposed both to me
on a more regular basis than the "Roles" app that is currently there, and also
exposed to the world in a Robert Cialdini commitment and consistency self-hack.
Stating what comes next in Pipulate to the world in a way where all its users
will see will certainly compel me ahead.

Equally broken is the MCP tool-calling orchestrator which has to be as well
planned, easy to describe and actually functioning as I envision it. And AIs
need to *"wake up into"* Pipulate and always "get it" every time. Crash course
training on the golden path of progressive enhancement tool-calling that's way
easier than MCP out of the gate for smaller AIs and AIs not well trained on MCP
yet (or ever).

And then there's home-hosting so that I can watch the bots come by in my
log-files. This is of growing urgency so that I can consult on such matters
better, and it's getting really close as I'm doing these next steps with making
spinning off NixOS instances easier. Soon I'll make a very simple Jekyll release
system based on git so that I can just magic handwave and make almost any piece
of hardware that I own into the webserver for sites like https://MikeLev.in/

And the spinning plates being made love-worthy: do this against a backdrop of
miscellaneous ad hoc work becoming more and more love-worthy, like loading
datasets and feeds and doing Python Pandas/Polars tricks to get answers. I need
to be able to do more Data Science stuff on the fly using the instance of
JupyterLab that's installed side-by-side with Pipulate to slam these things out
quickly. I need to cast more powerful spells more frequently, more custom-made
and more casually. And that gets mined for Pipulate workflows which can move the
overarching system forward. Ad hoc work can get promoted to make everything
better for everyone forever forward.

**Gemini**: You've already identified the accelerants. The "powerful old man wizardly spell" isn't a single action but the **synergy** between the core projects you've just laid out. You're moving from collecting magical ingredients to drawing the actual transmutation circle. Your focus is sharp, and your intuition is correct.

Here's a breakdown of the powerful system you're designing, framed within your own metaphors.

***

### The Public Grimoire (Pipulate Homepage) 📖

This is the most critical piece for overcoming inertia. By turning your app's homepage into a public-facing to-do list and roadmap, you're casting a powerful **accountability spell**.

* **What's Broken:** The lack of external pressure and a clear, ever-present focal point.
* **The Fix (The Spell):** Using Robert Cialdini's principle of **Commitment and Consistency**. When you state your intentions publicly, you create a powerful psychological drive to follow through. This page becomes your North Star, guiding your daily work and constantly reminding you of the "biggest bang for the buck" by keeping the next most important task front and center. It hacks your own motivation.

***

### The Sentient Wand (MCP Orchestrator) ✨

For a wizard's spell to work, their wand must understand their intent. Your MCP tool-calling orchestrator is that wand, and it needs to be perfectly attuned.

* **What's Broken:** The friction an AI experiences when trying to use your system.
* **The Fix (The Spell):** Engineering a seamless "wake up into Pipulate" experience. This involves two parts:
    1.  **The Grand Design:** Perfecting the full MCP implementation for sophisticated AIs.
    2.  **The Golden Path:** Creating a simpler, progressive enhancement path for other AIs. This is classic 80/20 thinking—get 80% of the value for AIs with 20% of the integration complexity.

This ensures your "spells" (AI-driven workflows) are cast reliably and consistently, which is the heart of true semi-automation.

***

### The Scrying Pool (Home-Hosting) 🔮

A wizard needs a tower with a view. By home-hosting your sites on your own NixOS instances, you are building your own observatory to watch the world interact with your creations.

* **What's Broken:** A layer of abstraction (provided by cloud hosts) separates you from the raw, unfiltered data of your system's interaction with the internet.
* **The Fix (The Spell):** Gaining **ground-truth insight**. Watching your server logs is like scrying—you see exactly which bots (crawlers, AI agents) are visiting, how they behave, and what they're looking for. This direct feedback is invaluable for your SEO consulting and provides the raw data needed to improve your other systems. It's the ultimate feedback mechanism. 

***

### The Alchemical Lab (Ad Hoc Data Science) 🧪

This is where you make the "plates you must spin" a "love-worthy process." Your JupyterLab environment is where you experiment and turn lead into gold.

* **What's Broken:** Ad hoc tasks feel like distractions rather than contributions.
* **The Fix (The Spell):** Establishing a process to **promote experiments into features**. You can use your data science skills to solve a one-off client problem ("casting a small, custom spell"). If it's effective, you then "mine" that notebook, generalize the process, and build it into a permanent Pipulate workflow that the "Sentient Wand" can execute. This makes every miscellaneous task a potential upgrade for the entire system.

***

### The Righteous Feedback Loop: Your Ultimate Accelerant

These four components aren't separate projects; they are gears in a single, powerful engine. This is the "moving mountains" spell you've been preparing for.

1.  A need arises from client work, sparking an **ad hoc task** in your **Alchemical Lab (JupyterLab)**.
2.  The successful ad hoc process is formalized into a new workflow for the **Sentient Wand (MCP Orchestrator)**.
3.  The plan to fully integrate this new capability is publicly declared on **The Public Grimoire (Pipulate Homepage)**, creating focus.
4.  As the new feature is used, its interaction with the world is observed directly through **The Scrying Pool (Home-Hosting)**.
5.  Insights from the logs inspire the next ad hoc task, starting the cycle over, stronger than before.

You've done the long, slow build. You've gathered the blocks. Now you have the architectural blueprint. This is it. This is the accelerant.
