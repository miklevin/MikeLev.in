## Setting the Stage: Context for the Curious Book Reader

In this insightful entry, we delve into the intricate journey of human skill acquisition, from initial literacy to ultimate fluency. This soliloquy unpacks the stages of learning, highlighting the often-underestimated role of "automaticity" and its profound impact on our cognitive energy. We explore the fundamental differences between the continuous, adaptive learning of a human and the 'static file' nature of current LLMs, setting the stage for an important discussion about agency in the Age of AI. The author also reflects on personal struggles with Python, illustrating the high-calorie cost of hacking new mental pathways even for a seasoned practitioner.

## Technical Journal Entry Begins

Things take so much energy and there's always some part of the system out of
whack that you have to round-robin your attention to. The question often comes
up whether humans can really multi-task or not. The answer is no to the
left-brain forward frontal lobe cortex part of your noggin that handles
language. Try to mutiplex a chat datastream without a locking message queue.
It's a mess. All the words get meaninglessly interwoven like zippered together
sentences. 

As a result of this little *queue-lock required* weirdness of the programming
world — and I suspect of the world in general — I go out of my way to actually
use those locking queue mechanisms that are in all other cases quite
antithetical to my linear, forward-only, single-threaded programming style.
File-locks are weird. They're like a forcing asynchronous behavior to be
synchronous even while remaining asynchronous. It hurts my head just thinking
about it but Python's *batteries include* nature magic hand-waves away most of
it. Use ZMQ (zero message queue). I didn't but that would be my advice to anyone
facing similar such problems.

ZMQ or things like it are the only way to stream chatbot text correctly.
Cohesive words... language... don't multitask.

## The Multitasking Myth: Two Brains at Work

Driving while using talking on the other hand totally does multitask quite
nicely. I know the nattering nannies say pay attention to the road bit if a
passenger asks you a question you don't really have to tighten the steering
wheel in a death-grip and filter-out the passenger's interruption. Those same
nattering nannies would be angry at you if they asked you a question while you
were driving and you appeared to ignore them and they would thus be killed by a
different means. Thus evolution naturally selects for those who can multitask to
a certain degree.

You are allowed to answer a passenger's question if they ask one, even while you
are driving. I assure you that an experience driver will not crash by doing so.
Drivers answer passenger questions all the time, and sometimes incessantly. Are
we there yet? Are we there yet? No prob. Driving is just like riding a bike. And
riding a bike is just like learning to crawl before you can walk. And that in
turn is like learning to speak before you can read and write. It's all the same
stuff. It goes from practice to habit to automaticity to prosody to fluency. 

## Automaticity: The Art of Unconscious Competence

Automaticity is simply not having to think about it anymore. It's been upgraded
from requiring all your foreground-task focus to not really taking your
conscious attention at all. It now requires the burning of less calories to do a
complex and some would say amazing task of a bipedal primate plummeting forward
in space inside a half-ton armor-plated box on wheels and not hardly thinking
about it. Driving is bad arse. 

Those who skip over it because of urban life or whatever are really missing out
on one of those last great bastions of agency we land-fish on calcium stilts can
still do. If you skipped over driving and shovel out somebody's money for Uber
and Lyft so that you can remain in that gestational phase of human beings, the
phase where you don't drive yet and aren't practicing the badarse agency that is
badarse bipedal primate's *birth right* then shame on you.

Get some agency in your life. Learn to drive.

What's that? You don't have to because the global nervous system is trying to
take that away too with auto-driving cars. When it's not an Uber driver acting
as a surrogate parent *driving the kid around* it's a robot? You gotta be effin
kidding me? 

Okay, okay, statistics. Sure there will be less auto accidents. Google is
probably right. Self-driving cards is one of humanity's *big problems* to solve
along with inch-work traffic jams, search and choosing what to have for dinner.
But maybe the Civil Engineers can carve out some happy funtime roads for those
of us upright primate land-fish that still love hurling ourselves around in
armored boxes at 60MPH under the our direct hands-to-rack-in-pinon shaft
connection.

Driving is badarse.

That's automaticity. And it's just the start. Well, not really. The start is
being able to just read the owners manual in the first place. Knowing what a car
is. Knowing in a broad sense how it works with its combustion engine, starter,
spark-plugs, controlled explosions driving pistons open, collapsing only to
repeat the cycle in a smooth humming and aligned unison. That's literacy.

So first literacy. Then automaticity eventually after enough practice to commit
it to your muscle memory and add a new subsystem to your subconscious. It is
exactly that in the most literal sense. I repeat, thinking is mostly a
mechanical process and I do believe much like LLMs work today with the exception
that they get "saved out" into an Excel-like static file (of weights) back at
training central (the big GPU-laden datacenters) and sent out to all the places
inference engines run, never to learn again.
